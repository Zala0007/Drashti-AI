# P04 synthetic performance report

Run date: 2026-08-27  
Host: development Windows workstation  
Command: `benchmark_stream_engine.py --counts 1 5 10 28 --duration 5`

## Method

Each stage launched actual concurrent FFmpeg processes against unique temporary
recorded-file paths. Sources were 320x180 at 10 FPS; P04 decoded at 8 FPS and
scheduled at 6 FPS. A P05-compatible consumer drained batches and performed 5 ms
of simulated work per batch. CPU and RSS include the Python process and all
child FFmpeg processes. GPU decode was unavailable.

| Streams | Decode FPS/stream | Dispatch FPS/stream | Avg frame age | Avg worker CPU | Peak RSS | Reconnects | Result |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 8.019 | 5.230 | 112.737 ms | 4.288% | 113.910 MB | 0 | Streaming |
| 5 | 8.043 | 5.228 | 124.297 ms | 17.423% | 330.414 MB | 0 | All streaming |
| 10 | 8.005 | 5.209 | 95.128 ms | 30.199% | 599.555 MB | 0 | All streaming |
| 28 | 8.007 | 5.216 | 89.097 ms | 68.025% | 1,571.953 MB | 0 | All streaming |

At 28 streams, 1,587 frames were received and 1,049 were consumed through the
batch interface during the measured/warm-up window. All 522 reported drops were
intentional `frames_sampled_out` due to the configured 8-to-6 FPS reduction;
there were no stale-frame or downstream-backpressure drops in this run.

The observed dispatch rate is below 6 FPS because a 30 ms scheduler tick cannot
land exactly on a 166.7 ms target interval; it dispatches on the next tick. This
is bounded and predictable. A deadline-based scheduler can reduce this quantized
gap if later model throughput requires it.

## Government evaluation grid qualification snapshot

On 2026-08-28 at 06:11 UTC, the configured catalogue returned 30 live entries.
Drishti admitted all 30 as isolated HLS-only P04 sessions, with a 1.5-second
stagger, 640x360 working frames, 4 FPS decode target, 3 FPS AI target, and a
45-second HTTP media watchdog. This was an operational snapshot, not a controlled
capacity benchmark.

| Sessions | Produced frames | State snapshot | Total frames | Reconnects | Worker CPU | RSS | Child/service processes |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 | 26 | 21 streaming, 4 connected, 1 degraded, 4 reconnecting | 12,993 | 170 | 71.9% | 4,417.7 MB | 53 |

The four zero-frame sessions were `GOV-LIVE-15`, `GOV-LIVE-17`,
`GOV-LIVE-18`, and `GOV-LIVE-22`. At the same observation time, provider-side
manifest checks showed a gap playlist for camera 15 and unavailable/timed-out
master playlists for 17, 18, and 22. Those feeds remained isolated and retrying;
the other sessions continued. Because provider state can change, rerun and
timestamp this check before an evaluation demonstration.

## What this proves

- actual independent decoder processes run concurrently;
- the latest-frame scheduler stays below the 750 ms age limit;
- the 28-session processing-unit admission target works at low resolution;
- the P05 batch consumer remains bounded and no reconnect occurred;
- shutdown releases source files and leaves no FFmpeg process behind.

## What this does not prove

This is not evidence for 28 production RTSP feeds, 1080p/H.265, packet loss,
real GPU inference, camera clock accuracy, or 80,000-camera statewide capacity.
Those require the government feeds and target edge hardware. Run the same matrix
with 1/5/10/28 unique real streams, record codec/bitrate, and add network receive,
GPU decode, model latency, accuracy, disconnect/recovery, and 8-24 hour soak data
before production sizing.
